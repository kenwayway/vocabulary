## Definition
To use language that can be read two ways on purpose, so that you never
have to commit to either — and can later claim you meant the harmless one.

## In the wild
> Asked three times whether the layoffs were finished, the chief executive
> equivocated, saying only that the company had "no further announcements
> planned at this time."

— *Financial Times*, 2026-05-02

## Etymology
Latin **aequus** ("equal") + **vocare** / **vox** ("to call", "voice") →
Late Latin *aequivocus*, "of equal voice", i.e. a word that calls out to two
meanings at once. Same **vox** root as *vocal*, *vocation*, *advocate*,
*revoke*, *provoke*. Same **aequus** root as *equal*, *equity*, *equanimity*,
*adequate*.

## Word family
equivocation (n.) · equivocal (adj., "open to two readings") ·
unequivocal (adj., "admitting no doubt" — the useful opposite)

## Synonyms & nuance
- **prevaricate** — closer to outright lying; you dodge by saying false
  things, where equivocating dodges by saying *ambiguous* things.
- **hedge** — neutral to mildly negative, and often sensible: you qualify a
  claim because you're genuinely uncertain. Equivocating is not honest doubt.
- **waffle** — informal; suggests indecisiveness and padding rather than
  cunning. A waffler can't decide; an equivocator has decided not to say.
- **be evasive** — plain and general; equivocate is specifically about the
  double meaning of the words themselves.

## Collocations
- equivocate about / over / on (a question)
- refuse to equivocate
- equivocate when pressed
- an unequivocal answer / denial / success

## My sentences
- I asked whether the deadline had moved, and he equivocated for a full
  minute without once using the word "no".

## Notes
The giveaway is that an equivocation is *technically true*. If you catch
yourself admiring how carefully a sentence was built, that's the word.
Remember it through **equal voice**: two meanings speaking at the same volume.
