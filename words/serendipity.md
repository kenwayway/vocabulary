## Definition
The knack of finding something valuable while you were looking for something
else entirely — luck and alertness together, not luck alone.

## In the wild
> Penicillin, the microwave oven and the Post-it note were all products of
> serendipity: someone noticed that a failed experiment had quietly succeeded
> at something else.

— *The Economist*, "Accidents of genius", 2026-06-14

## Etymology
Coined by Horace Walpole in a letter of 1754, from the Persian fairy tale
*The Three Princes of Serendip* (Serendip being an old name for Sri Lanka).
Walpole's princes "were always making discoveries, by accidents and sagacity,
of things which they were not in quest of." That last clause is the whole
word: **accident + sagacity**. Pure luck is not serendipity — you have to be
sharp enough to notice what you stumbled into.

## Word family
serendipitous (adj.) · serendipitously (adv.)

## Synonyms & nuance
- **fluke** — informal, and grudging: a fluke is luck you don't deserve, often
  used to dismiss someone's success ("a lucky fluke").
- **happy accident** — plain and neutral, describes the *outcome*; serendipity
  describes the *faculty* of turning accidents into outcomes.
- **kismet** — fate, destiny. Implies the universe meant it; serendipity
  implies nobody meant anything.
- **providence** — same idea but with a religious grain: benevolent design.

## Collocations
- pure / sheer / happy serendipity
- a moment of serendipity
- by serendipity
- rely on / leave it to serendipity

## My sentences
- Half of what I know about this codebase I learned by serendipity, while
  hunting down an unrelated bug.

## Notes
Do not use it to mean "good luck" flatly — that drains the word. The
discovery has to be *unsought*. If you were looking for it, it isn't
serendipity, it's just success.
