## Definition
So thin or slight that it might not hold — used of connections, claims,
grips and holds rather than of physical objects.

## In the wild
> The link between the two studies is tenuous at best: they measured
> different populations, over different periods, using different definitions
> of "recovery".

— *Nature*, correspondence, 2026-04-19

## Etymology
Latin **tenuis**, "thin, slender, fine" — the same root as *attenuate*
(to thin something out), *extenuate* (to thin down a fault, hence
*extenuating circumstances*), and, at a distance, *tender* and *thin*
themselves through Proto-Indo-European **ten-**, "to stretch". The image is
of something stretched so far that it is about to fail.

## Word family
tenuously (adv.) · tenuousness (n.) · attenuate (v.) · extenuate (v.)

## Synonyms & nuance
- **flimsy** — blunter and more dismissive; a flimsy excuse is one nobody
  believed for a second. Tenuous is the polite academic version.
- **precarious** — about danger, not thinness: a precarious position may
  collapse and hurt you. A tenuous link just fails to persuade.
- **slender** — mostly literal and often positive (a slender figure); of
  evidence it's a near synonym but sounds gentler.
- **shaky** — informal, suggests wobble and lack of confidence; tenuous
  suggests an argument stretched too far to bear weight.

## Collocations
- a tenuous link / connection / grasp / hold
- tenuous at best
- tenuous evidence / claim / argument
- cling to a tenuous...

## My sentences
- My grasp of the deployment pipeline is tenuous enough that I would rather
  not be the one to push on a Friday.

## Notes
Almost always abstract. "A tenuous thread" works because a thread is already
a metaphor for a connection; "a tenuous chair" does not. Hold onto the root
image: **stretched thin**.
